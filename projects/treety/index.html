02 / The project

An idea with a purpose.

Treety is an underground kit that keeps street-tree roots from lifting the sidewalk and keeps new street trees watered. A buried box captures curb runoff all winter and feeds it to the tree through the dry season by sand wicks, 12 to 18 inches down on the curb side, so that is where the roots grow.

It stays in the ground for good.

The box is permanent. After about five years an established tree may not need its water, but the box keeps protecting the sidewalk by holding the roots on the curb side, or switches to recharge mode and sends each storm down toward groundwater. There is an option to keep the existing sidewalk with no structural soil underneath, so a city can add Treety without rebuilding the walk.

Where it stands.

I lead a team of two, funded through USC’s six-week SHIP program for student founders on the Social Impact track. Two pieces are in build: a wick-bench prototype (sand wicks, evaporation trays, constant-level water bottles on scales) that measures how much water a wick can lift 24 inches day after day, and a simulation of what roots do today without the kit: sidewalk damage, sewer and water line intrusion, and foundation damage.

Pilot cities.

Three cities have offered public works contacts for a pilot of two to three trees through one dry season, May to October. Status is updated as conversations move.
