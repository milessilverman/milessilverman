1 · Result

2-inch PVC, with 2½-inch for the last 841 m

The survey puts the spring only 13.1 m above the tank site, about 11 m less than the satellite estimate. With the inlet 2.5 m above the tank floor, 10.6 m of drop is available. An all-2-inch line would use 9.7 m of it at 0.8 L/s, leaving 0.9 m: under the 1 m minimum, and below zero once the pipe ages. Switching the last 841 m (station 70 to the tank) to 2½-inch cuts the loss to 8.3 m and leaves 2.3 m, or 1.1 m with aged pipe, for about 10% more PVC.

Two things to confirm before buying pipe

The model takes the spring box water level as the benchmark (station 1). If the water sits lower than the benchmark, subtract the difference from the 10.6 m. The dry-season spring flow is still unmeasured: if the spring gives 0.7 L/s or less, an all-2-inch line has enough margin (section 5).

2 · Survey

What the total station changed

The survey has 96 points. Station 2 ("North") is the orientation shot beside the benchmark and is not on the pipe, which leaves 95 points from the spring to the tank site. Every point lies within 30 m of the GPS track (half within 9 m), so the survey follows the same alignment.

Stations 72 to 83 were recorded out of route order, which makes the sheet's running total jump back and forth. The sheet totals 4,071 m; in route order the line is 3,050 m measured flat, matching the GPS track's 3,048 m, and 3,143 m along the ground, including the 2.5 m riser up the tank wall. The model uses the route order shown in the appendix.

Elevations stay in the survey's own datum, with the benchmark at 1000.000 m. The sheet's "GPS Elevation" column adds 1,558.199 m, the handheld GPS reading at the benchmark; that reading was about 43 m low, so treat the column as relative heights, not true altitude.

3 · Design flow

0.8 L/s on the busiest day of 2048

Unchanged from Rev A. The line is sized for the whole community's busiest-day demand at the end of a 20-year design period, following Guatemalan rural water criteria (INFOM). If the spring gives less in the dry season, the line carries whatever the spring gives.

4 · Head loss

How much drop each pipe size needs

Worked example: the recommended line at 0.8 L/s

  1. 2-inch section, 2,302 m. Water moves at 0.33–0.39 m/s. Hazen-Williams (C = 140) gives about 3.2 m of loss per km: 7.42 m including fittings.
  2. 2½-inch section, 841 m. The wider bore slows the water to 0.22 m/s, and the loss drops to about 1 m per km: 0.91 m including the exit into the tank.
  3. Total. 8.33 m used of 10.6 m available, so the inlet keeps 2.27 m of pressure. With aged pipe (C = 130) it keeps 1.07 m.

5 · Options

Three ways to build it

All 2-inchRecommended mixAll 2½-inch

When all-2-inch is enough

  • Spring gives 0.7 L/s or less. At 0.7 L/s the inlet keeps 3.0 m (1.9 m with aged pipe).
  • Tank inlet 1.0 m above the tank site instead of 2.5 m (a half-buried tank). The inlet keeps 2.4 m (1.0 m aged), but the tank's water sits 1.5 m lower, so every home downhill gets 1.5 m less pressure.

Why the 2½-inch pipe goes at the tank end

Upsizing saves the same head wherever it goes, so it belongs where the larger pipe is cheapest. From station 61 to the tank the no-flow pressure stays under 72 m, so the 2½-inch pipe can be the thin-walled 160 psi class. Upsizing in the valley would mean thick 250 or 315 psi pipe.

Water hammer

A pressure wave crosses the line and returns in about 15 seconds. Each class was chosen so no-flow pressure plus a sudden-closure spike at 0.7 m/s stays within the pipe's rating; the real full-open speed is only 0.44 m/s, which leaves extra margin.

ClassWave speedSpike at full flowMax no-flow allowed

Use gate valves, not quarter-turn ball valves, and close each over a minute or more.

Low velocity

At 0.8 L/s the water moves at 0.33–0.39 m/s in the 2-inch pipe and 0.22 m/s in the 2½-inch section, below the 0.6 m/s guides ask for to keep sediment moving. No size reaches 0.6 m/s with a 10.6 m drop, so handle sediment with a sand trap at the spring box, washouts at the low points, and periodic flushing with the tank valve wide open.

8 · Valves and crossings

Placed from the survey's high and low points

Main valves go at every high or low point that stands out by 5 m or more. The minor points stand out by 2.5–3.6 m; grade the trench to smooth them out, or add a valve if the ground won't allow it. Each valve must be rated for the no-flow pressure listed.

9 · Model files

Using the EPANET files

Vicampanavitz_conduction_SURVEY_design_0.8Ls.inp

Design run. SPRING is a reservoir at the benchmark (1000.000 m); TANK_IN draws 0.8 L/s at 989.396 m. Nodes are named by survey station (S3 to S96) and sit at their total station coordinates, so the EPANET map matches the survey.

Vicampanavitz_conduction_SURVEY_capacity_check.inp

Capacity run. TANK_IN becomes a free-discharge reservoir, and EPANET solves how much the line carries fully open. These two files replace the Rev A files built on satellite elevations.

  1. Open a file in EPANET 2.2 (free from the US EPA) and click Run.
  2. If the spring box water level differs from the benchmark, change the SPRING head.
  3. To test a different tank height, change the TANK_IN elevation; to test a flow, change its demand.
10 · Appendix

Method and station results

  • Solver: EPANET 2.2 engine, steady state, SI units. A separate hand calculation of the delivered file gives the same 8.33 m and 0.91 L/s.
  • Route order: each survey point placed by its position along the GPS track (the survey grid matches the GPS track to within 1.5° of rotation).
  • Pipe lengths: 3D distance between consecutive stations, plus a 2.5 m riser at the tank.
  • Friction: Hazen-Williams, C = 140; fittings K = 0.3 per pipe, plus entrance 0.5, two gate valves 0.2 each, exit 1.0.
  • Pressure class: no-flow pressure plus a sudden-closure spike at 0.7 m/s within the ASTM D2241 rating. A 124 m stretch (S39–S46) that could take 160 psi is folded into the surrounding 250 psi run to keep the schedule simple.
  • Inside diameters (Amanco Wavin): 2″ 55.70 / 53.21 / 51.38 mm and 2½″ 67.44 mm for 160 / 250 / 315 psi.
