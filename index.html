02 / Transportation & cities

Trains, streets,
and the land around them.

Cutting intercity emissions by building on corridors that already exist, and paying for it with the value that stations create.

Corridor map coming soonA self-made map of candidate high-speed rail corridors and stations, built mostly on existing rights-of-way: BNSF through the Central Valley, freeway medians, and existing rail lines, to cut cost.
Placeholder · map in progress
Course report · CE 106Report

Long-distance and intercity trains

My CE 106 technical report on how the U.S. could cut intercity emissions with rail that fits the corridors it already has.

  • Partly electrified hybrid track, with full high-speed rail only on key corridors.
  • Overnight sleepers for long haul, commuter trains for short haul.
  • Auto trains, so you wake up with your car in another city.
  • Value capture around stations, such as EIFDs, to pay for it.

04 / Concepts

Early-stage ideas.

Ideas I’m sketching before they become projects. Nothing here is built yet.

Concept

AI street sweeper

A street sweeper that cleans the street, sorts the trash it collects, and scans the city as it goes for infrastructure flaws: cracks, clogged drains, failing curbs. One vehicle that already drives every block becomes the inspection fleet. It is part of a broader effort to repair and restore urban infrastructure before small problems become expensive ones.

07 / A little about me

Cities are systems.
People are the point.

I’m a first-year civil engineering student at USC Viterbi, class of 2030, planning a minor in real estate development through USC Price.

Growing up in Los Angeles made me curious about the infrastructure around me: the trains, bridges, streets, and networks that hold a city together. It also made me aware of how much we ask of those systems when something goes wrong.

I want to connect safer streets, reliable transportation, greenery, and welcoming public spaces with the value created by thoughtful development. By bringing engineering, design, and real estate together, I hope to make better places financially achievable and put that value back into the communities around them.

I’m drawn to urban planning, sustainability, environmental engineering, and public space design. On the side I run Weblane, a web design business for small businesses.

My ultimate goal is to improve quality of life in cities, with real estate development helping subsidize the infrastructure and public spaces that make everyday life better.
